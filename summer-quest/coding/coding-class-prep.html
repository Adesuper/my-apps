FIRST THING

🤔 What is coding?

Coding means giving a computer a list of instructions to follow. A computer is powerful but not clever on its own — it does exactly what you tell it, in exactly the order you say it.

You are the boss. The computer is the worker. 👷

If you tell it the wrong step, it will happily do the wrong thing! That's why coders think carefully about every step. Finding and fixing a wrong step is called debugging 🐛.

BIG WORD, EASY IDEA

📋 What is an algorithm?

An algorithm is just a step-by-step plan to get something done — like a recipe. You already use algorithms every day!

Brushing your teeth is an algorithm:

  1. Pick up the toothbrush
  2. Put toothpaste on it
  3. Brush your teeth
  4. Rinse your mouth

Swap two steps and it goes wrong — you can't brush before the toothpaste is on! Order matters. That's the #1 rule coders live by.

MEET YOUR TOOL

🐱 What is Scratch?

Scratch is the app you'll use in class. Instead of typing code, you snap colourful blocks together like LEGO. Each block is one instruction, and they stack to make an algorithm.

The characters you control are called sprites (the cat is the famous one 🐱). You press the green flag 🟢 to start your program.

A block stack looks like this:

when 🟢 clicked
say "Hello!" for 2 seconds
move 10 steps
wait 1 second
say "I can code!"

Read it top to bottom — that's the algorithm the cat will follow. See? You can already read Scratch!

THE CLASS PROJECT

🎬 Your first project: an Animated Greeting

By the end of Wednesday you'll make a sprite that says hello and moves — your own little animation. If you want to brainstorm now:

  1. What should your sprite say? (e.g. "Hi, I'm [your name]!")
  2. Should it move, spin, or change colour?
  3. What background would look cool?

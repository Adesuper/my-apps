1 🔢 Place value

🎯 Objective: read and write numbers to the ten-thousands and state the value of each digit.

The idea

Each digit has a place: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands. A digit's value = the digit × its place. The same digit is worth different amounts depending on where it sits.

On the board

3,742 → 3 thousands, 7 hundreds, 4 tens, 2 ones
The 7 is worth 700, not 7.
✏️ Activity: build 2,516 with base-ten blocks (or draw squares=100, lines=10, dots=1). Then ask “what is the 5 worth?”

Exercises

  1. In 5,281, what is the value of the 2?
  2. Which digit is in the hundreds place of 4,073?
  3. Write “six thousand, four hundred nine” in digits.
  4. What number is 4 thousands, 0 hundreds, 5 tens, 6 ones?
Show answers

1. 200 2. 0 3. 6,409 4. 4,056

▶ Practice: “Place value”

2 🧱 Expanded form & comparing

🎯 Objective: write a number in expanded form and compare/order numbers with <, >, =.

The idea

Expanded form = the sum of each digit's value. To compare, line up the places and read from the left; the first place where they differ decides. > means “greater”, < means “less”.

On the board

3,742 = 3000 + 700 + 40 + 2
3,742 > 3,724 (same thousands & hundreds; 4 tens beats 2 tens)
✏️ Activity: the “left-to-right duel” — write two 4-digit numbers, race to spot the first place that differs.

Exercises

  1. Write 2,650 in expanded form.
  2. Put <, > or =: 4,318 __ 4,381
  3. Put <, > or =: 7,900 __ 7,900
  4. Order from least to greatest: 1,205 · 1,052 · 1,250
Show answers

1. 2000 + 600 + 50 2. < 3. = 4. 1,052 · 1,205 · 1,250

▶ Practice: “Compare & order”

3 🎯 Estimating & rounding

🎯 Objective: round to the nearest ten/hundred and estimate to check if an answer is reasonable.

The idea

To round, look at the digit to the right of the place you're rounding to: 5 or more → round up, 4 or less → stay. Estimating = round first, then add/subtract the round numbers.

On the board

47 → nearest ten = 50 · 823 → nearest hundred = 800
Estimate 38 + 41 ≈ 40 + 40 = 80
✏️ Activity: use a number line 40–50 and mark 47 — which ten is it closer to?

Exercises

  1. Round 63 to the nearest ten.
  2. Round 85 to the nearest ten.
  3. Round 472 to the nearest hundred.
  4. Estimate 29 + 52 by rounding to tens.
Show answers

1. 60 2. 90 3. 500 4. 30 + 50 = 80

▶ Practice: “Rounding”

4 ⚖️ Even/odd, prime/composite

🎯 Objective: sort numbers as even/odd and as prime/composite.

The idea

Even numbers end in 0,2,4,6,8 (they split into two equal groups); odd end in 1,3,5,7,9. A prime has exactly two factors (1 and itself). A composite has more than two. (1 is neither.)

On the board

24 → even (ends in 4) · 7 → prime (only 1×7)
12 → composite (factors 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12)
✏️ Activity: try to make an even array (2 equal rows) for 7 counters — it won't work → 7 is odd & prime.

Exercises

  1. Even or odd? a) 38 b) 51
  2. Is 9 prime or composite?
  3. Is 11 prime or composite?
  4. List all the factors of 8.
Show answers

1. a) even b) odd 2. composite (1, 3, 9) 3. prime 4. 1, 2, 4, 8

▶ Practice: “Number types”

5 🔁 Patterns & counting big sets

🎯 Objective: find and extend a number pattern; count a large set by grouping.

The idea

A pattern follows a rule (add, subtract, or multiply the same each time). Find the rule, then extend it. To count many objects, group into equal bunches (tens or fives) and skip-count.

On the board

4, 8, 12, 16, __ → rule +4 → 20
100, 90, 80, __ → rule −10 → 70
✏️ Activity: pour out a handful of beans; group by tens and skip-count 10, 20, 30…

Exercises

  1. Next number: 5, 10, 15, 20, __
  2. Next number: 30, 27, 24, __
  3. What is the rule for 2, 4, 8, 16? What comes next?
  4. 6 bags of 10 marbles = __ marbles
Show answers

1. 25 2. 21 3. doubling (×2); next is 32 4. 60

▶ Practice: “Patterns”

6 ➕ +/− facts & mental math

🎯 Objective: recall addition/subtraction facts to 20 using smart strategies.

The idea

Strategies beat memorising: make 10 (bridging), doubles & near-doubles, +10 / −10, and using a fact you know. Fact families link + and − (7+5=12 ⇒ 12−5=7).

On the board

8 + 5 → 8 + 2 = 10, then + 3 = 13
7 + 6 → double 6 is 12, + 1 = 13
15 − 9 → 15 − 10 = 5, then + 1 = 6
✏️ Activity: flash 10 fact cards; for each, ask “which strategy did you use?”

Exercises

  1. Make-10: 9 + 6 = __
  2. Near double: 6 + 7 = __
  3. 14 − 8 = __
  4. 7 + 5 = 12, so 12 − 5 = __
Show answers

1. 15 2. 13 3. 6 4. 7

▶ Practice: “+/− facts”

7 🧮 Big +/− with regrouping

🎯 Objective: add and subtract 3-digit numbers with carrying and borrowing.

The idea

Line numbers up by place. Work right to left. Carry when a column makes 10+ (10 ones become 1 ten). Borrow when the top digit is smaller than the bottom (1 ten becomes 10 ones).

On the board

348 + 276: 8+6=14 (write 4, carry 1) · 4+7+1=12 (write 2, carry 1) · 3+2+1=6 → 624
503 − 178: borrow to get → 325
✏️ Activity: do 348 + 276 once with base-ten blocks, trading 10 ones for a ten, before the written way.

Exercises

  1. 265 + 137 = __
  2. 428 + 385 = __
  3. 640 − 275 = __
  4. 700 − 458 = __
Show answers

1. 402 2. 813 3. 365 4. 242

▶ Practice: “Big +/−”

8 ❓ Missing terms & equivalent expressions

🎯 Objective: find an unknown in an equation, and tell whether two expressions are equal.

The idea

To find a missing term, use the inverse operation (undo it). To add, subtract back; to subtract, add back. Two expressions are equivalent if they have the same value.

On the board

7 + __ = 12 → 12 − 7 = 5
__ − 4 = 9 → 9 + 4 = 13
Is 4 + 5 = 3 + 6? both are 9 → yes
✏️ Activity: use a balance scale (or drawing) — both sides must weigh the same.

Exercises

  1. 6 + __ = 15
  2. __ − 8 = 7
  3. 20 − __ = 12
  4. True or false: 5 + 7 = 6 + 6
Show answers

1. 9 2. 15 3. 8 4. true (both are 12)

▶ Practice: “Missing terms”

9 ✖️ Multiplication & the tables

🎯 Objective: understand × as equal groups/arrays and recall the tables to 10.

The idea

Multiplication is equal groups: 4 × 3 = 4 groups of 3 = 12. An array (rows × columns) shows the same thing — and shows why 4 × 3 = 3 × 4. Learn tables in order: 2, 5, 10 → 4 → 3 → 9 → squares → 6, 7, 8.

On the board

3 × 4 = 3 rows of 4 dots = 12
Only the “hard” facts to drill: 3×6, 3×7, 3×8, 4×6, 4×7, 4×8, 6×7, 6×8, 7×8
✏️ Activity: build 4 × 6 as an array of dots, then turn the paper 90° — it becomes 6 × 4, same total.

Exercises

  1. 3 groups of 5 = __
  2. 7 × 4 = __
  3. 6 × 8 = __
  4. If 8 × 3 = 24, what is 3 × 8?
Show answers

1. 15 2. 28 3. 48 4. 24 (order doesn't change the product)

▶ Practice: “Times tables”

10 ➗ Division

🎯 Objective: understand ÷ as equal sharing/grouping and use the ×↔÷ link.

The idea

Division means share equally (12 ÷ 3 = share 12 into 3 groups → 4 each) or make groups (how many 3s in 12? → 4). It's the opposite of ×: 3 × 4 = 12, so 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

On the board

15 ÷ 3 = 5 · 20 ÷ 4 = 5
Fact family: 6 × 7 = 42 → 42 ÷ 6 = 7 → 42 ÷ 7 = 6
✏️ Activity: deal 18 cards equally to 3 players — how many each? Now to 6 players?

Exercises

  1. 18 ÷ 3 = __
  2. 28 ÷ 4 = __
  3. 35 ÷ 5 = __
  4. 6 × 9 = 54, so 54 ÷ 9 = __
Show answers

1. 6 2. 7 3. 7 4. 6

▶ Practice: “Division”

11 ✳️ Multi-digit multiplication

🎯 Objective: multiply a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number.

The idea

Split the 2-digit number into tens + ones (the area method), multiply each part, then add. 23 × 4 = (20 × 4) + (3 × 4) = 80 + 12 = 92.

On the board

23 × 4: 20 × 4 = 80, 3 × 4 = 12, 80 + 12 = 92
Written: 4 × 3 = 12 (write 2, carry 1); 4 × 2 = 8, + 1 = 9 → 92
✏️ Activity: draw a 2-box area model (tens box, ones box) for 15 × 4 and fill each box.

Exercises

  1. 13 × 3 = __
  2. 24 × 2 = __
  3. 15 × 4 = __
  4. 32 × 3 = __
Show answers

1. 39 2. 48 3. 60 4. 96

▶ Practice: “Multi-digit ×”

12 🍕 Fractions

🎯 Objective: read a fraction, compare fractions, and recognise equivalent fractions.

The idea

A fraction is equal parts of one whole. The bottom (denominator) = how many equal parts the whole is cut into; the top (numerator) = how many you take. With the same bottom, the bigger top is bigger. Equivalent fractions name the same amount (1/2 = 2/4).

On the board

3/4 = 3 of 4 equal parts shaded
1/4 < 3/4 · but 1/8 < 1/4 (eighths are smaller pieces!) · 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6
✏️ Activity: fold a paper strip in halves, then quarters — show 1/2 = 2/4 by lining them up.

Exercises

  1. A pizza is cut into 8 equal slices; you eat 3. What fraction did you eat?
  2. Which is bigger: 2/5 or 4/5?
  3. Which is bigger: 1/3 or 1/6?
  4. Fill in: 1/2 = __/4
Show answers

1. 3/8 2. 4/5 3. 1/3 4. 2/4 (top = 2)

▶ Practice: “Fractions”

13 💵 Decimals & money

🎯 Objective: read decimals (tenths/hundredths), link them to money and fractions, and add/subtract them.

The idea

Decimals show parts of a whole: 0.1 = 1/10, 0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2. Money is the easy doorway: $3.75 = 3 dollars and 75 cents. To add/subtract, line up the decimal points.
🌍 At school he sees a comma: « 3,75 ». Tell him 3,75 (school) = 3.75 (here) — same number.

On the board

0.5 = 1/2 · $2.50 + $1.25 = $3.75 · 0.7 + 0.2 = 0.9
✏️ Activity: use real coins — show $0.50 as two quarters, and 0.5 as half a dollar.

Exercises

  1. Write 0.5 as a fraction.
  2. $4.25 + $3.50 = __
  3. $10.00 − $6.75 = __
  4. Which is more: 0.6 or 0.60?
Show answers

1. 1/2 (or 5/10) 2. $7.75 3. $3.25 4. they are equal

▶ Practice: “Decimals & money”

14 🦋 Symmetry & reflection

🎯 Objective: find lines of symmetry, reflect a shape, and recognise a repeating frieze.

The idea

A shape has a line of symmetry if it folds onto itself exactly. A reflection is a mirror flip across a line. A frieze is a border pattern that repeats by sliding.

On the board

Square → 4 lines of symmetry · “A” → 1 (vertical) · “S” → 0
Reflect a flag shape across a vertical mirror line.
✏️ Activity: fold letters cut from paper to test symmetry; make a frieze by stamping the same shape along a line.

Exercises

  1. How many lines of symmetry does a square have?
  2. Does the letter “H” have a line of symmetry?
  3. Does the letter “R” have a line of symmetry?
  4. A frieze repeats by ___ (sliding / growing)?
Show answers

1. 4 2. yes (2: vertical & horizontal) 3. no 4. sliding

▶ Practice: “Geometry”

15 📍 The coordinate grid

🎯 Objective: locate and name points on a grid with an ordered pair.

The idea

A point is named by an ordered pair (across, then up). Start at 0, go right the first number, then up the second: (right, up).

On the board

(3, 2) → 3 right, 2 up · Remember: across before up (“crawl before you climb”).
✏️ Activity: play “treasure grid” — hide a star at a pair and have him find it, then swap roles.

Exercises

  1. In (4, 1), which number says how far to go RIGHT?
  2. Start at 0; go 2 right and 5 up. Write the pair.
  3. Point A is 0 right and 3 up. Write it.
  4. Which comes first in a pair — across or up?
Show answers

1. 4 2. (2, 5) 3. (0, 3) 4. across (right)

▶ Practice: “Geometry”

16 📐 Angles & lines

🎯 Objective: identify right/acute/obtuse angles and parallel/perpendicular lines.

The idea

A right angle is a square corner (like the corner of a page). Smaller = acute, bigger = obtuse. Parallel lines never meet (train tracks); perpendicular lines cross at a right angle (a “+”).

On the board

Corner of paper = right angle · ‖ train tracks = parallel · + = perpendicular
✏️ Activity: use the corner of a card to test which classroom angles are right, smaller, or bigger.

Exercises

  1. What do we call a “square corner” angle?
  2. Parallel or perpendicular: railway tracks?
  3. Parallel or perpendicular: the two lines of a “+”?
  4. An angle smaller than a right angle is called ___.
Show answers

1. a right angle 2. parallel 3. perpendicular 4. acute

▶ Practice: “Geometry”

17 ⬡ Polygons

🎯 Objective: recognise and name polygons by their number of sides.

The idea

A polygon is a closed shape made only of straight sides. Named by sides: triangle (3), quadrilateral (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6). A circle is not a polygon — it's curved.

On the board

△ triangle = 3 sides · ▢ quadrilateral = 4 · ⬠ pentagon = 5 · ⬡ hexagon = 6
✏️ Activity: hunt for polygons around the room and count each one's sides.

Exercises

  1. How many sides does a pentagon have?
  2. A shape with 4 straight sides is a ___.
  3. Is a circle a polygon?
  4. How many sides does a hexagon have?
Show answers

1. 5 2. quadrilateral 3. no (it is curved) 4. 6

▶ Practice: “Geometry”

18 📦 Solids & nets

🎯 Objective: describe prisms and pyramids and recognise their nets.

The idea

Prisms have two identical ends and rectangle sides (a cereal box, a can). Pyramids have one base and triangle sides meeting at a point. A net is the solid unfolded flat.

On the board

Cube → 6 square faces; its net = 6 squares
Rectangular prism = box · Triangular pyramid = 4 triangle faces
✏️ Activity: unfold an empty box and lay it flat — that flat shape is its net; count the faces.

Exercises

  1. How many faces does a cube have?
  2. A pyramid's side faces are shaped like ___.
  3. The flat, unfolded version of a solid is called a ___.
  4. A cereal box is a ___ (prism / pyramid)?
Show answers

1. 6 2. triangles 3. net 4. prism (a rectangular prism)

▶ Practice: “Geometry”

19 📏 Length, perimeter & area

🎯 Objective: measure length (metric), and tell perimeter apart from area.

The idea

Length: mm, cm, m, km (100 cm = 1 m). Perimeter = the distance all the way around the edge (add every side). Area = the space inside, counted in squares.

On the board

Rectangle 4 cm by 3 cm:
Perimeter = 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14 cm · Area = 4 × 3 = 12 squares
✏️ Activity: on grid paper, draw a rectangle; walk a finger around the edge (perimeter) then colour the squares inside (area).

Exercises

  1. 1 metre = ___ cm
  2. Perimeter of a square with side 5 cm?
  3. Area of a 6-by-2 rectangle (in squares)?
  4. Perimeter of a rectangle 7 cm by 3 cm?
Show answers

1. 100 2. 20 cm 3. 12 4. 20 cm

▶ Practice: “Measurement”

20 ⏰ Time

🎯 Objective: know time units and read an analog clock.

The idea

60 seconds = 1 minute · 60 minutes = 1 hour · 24 hours = 1 day · 7 days = 1 week · 12 months = 1 year. On a clock, the short hand = hour, the long hand = minutes, and each number = 5 minutes.

On the board

Long hand on 3 → 15 minutes · Long hand on 6 → 30 minutes (half past)
✏️ Activity: set a paper clock to “3:30”, “quarter past 4”, and “10 to 6”.

Exercises

  1. How many minutes in 1 hour?
  2. The long hand points to 6 — how many minutes past the hour?
  3. How many days in a week?
  4. It is 4:00. In 30 minutes it will be ___.
Show answers

1. 60 2. 30 3. 7 4. 4:30

▶ Practice: “Measurement”

21 🧴 Volume, capacity & mass

🎯 Objective: choose sensible units for capacity and mass, and understand volume.

The idea

Capacity = how much a container holds (mL, L; 1000 mL = 1 L). Mass = how heavy (g, kg; 1000 g = 1 kg). Volume = the space a solid takes up (count unit cubes).

On the board

Water bottle ≈ 500 mL · 1 L = 1000 mL · Bag of sugar = 1 kg = 1000 g
A tower of 8 unit cubes → volume = 8
✏️ Activity: pour a 500 mL bottle twice into a 1 L jug — show 500 + 500 = 1000 mL = 1 L.

Exercises

  1. 1 litre = ___ mL
  2. 1 kilogram = ___ g
  3. Which unit for the mass of an apple: g or kg?
  4. Which holds more: 2 L or 1500 mL?
Show answers

1. 1000 2. 1000 3. grams (g) 4. 2 L (= 2000 mL)

▶ Practice: “Measurement”

22 📊 Data & probability

🎯 Objective: read tables/bar graphs, judge likelihood, and count combinations.

The idea

A table or bar graph organises data — the tallest bar shows the most. Probability is how likely something is: certain · likely · unlikely · impossible. Combinations: multiply the choices (2 shirts × 3 shorts = 6 outfits).

On the board

Tallest bar = most popular · Rolling a 7 on a 1–6 die = impossible
2 breads × 3 fillings = 6 sandwiches
✏️ Activity: list all outfits from 2 tops and 3 bottoms — count them, then check with 2 × 3.

Exercises

  1. On a bar graph, the tallest bar shows the ___ (most / least).
  2. Rolling a 7 on a normal die is ___ (certain / impossible).
  3. A bag has 9 red and 1 blue marble. Drawing red is ___ (likely / unlikely).
  4. 2 kinds of bread × 3 fillings = ___ sandwiches.
Show answers

1. most 2. impossible 3. likely 4. 6

▶ Practice: “Data & probability”

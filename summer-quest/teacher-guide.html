CONTEXT

About the learner

  • Level: Entering Grade 3 (3ᵉ année), following the Québec program. French is a school subject taught in French.
  • Ability: Capable and quick — please pitch to his real level, not a baby level. He does best when the work is a genuine (but reachable) challenge.
  • Motivation: Responds well to games, quick wins, points, and being told why a rule works. Keep sessions lively; short and focused beats long and heavy.
  • Goal for now: A confident head start on the Grade 3 grammar, conjugation and vocabulary so class feels like review, not first contact.
✅ What works best: teach one idea, show one clear example, then let him practise 5 questions on the page. After a correct answer, ask “pourquoi ?” — explaining it out loud is what makes it stick.

📖 Vocabulary & reading (Vocabulaire & lecture)

  • Vocabulary skills: word meaning, word families, synonyms, homophones, theme words, using the dictionary → quiz topic 📖 Le vocabulaire.
  • Reading strategies (from the dossiers): time & place in a story, the main character & their actions, chronological order, the subject/aspects of a text, appreciating a text, relation markers. Best taught with a short text (use the workbook readings or the app's reading land).
  • Writing (Écriture): descriptive card, a letter, the start of a story, a short poem, an appreciation card. Great as an end-of-week task once the grammar is in place.

SECTION 4

How to run a 30–40 minute session

  1. Warm-up (3 min): one quick quiz round on last time's topic.
  2. Teach (8–10 min): open the matching study card, show one clear example, then a second one he helps you finish.
  3. Guided practice (5 min): do the first quiz round together, reading each question aloud (there's a 🔊 tap-to-hear on the page).
  4. Independent practice (10 min): he plays 2–3 rounds of the topic on his own. You watch for patterns in his mistakes.
  5. Check & close (5 min): ask him to teach you the rule in his own words, and write one sentence using it (his “exit ticket”).
🎯 If he gets 4–5/5 twice in a row on a topic, he's ready to move on. If he's stuck below 3/5, reteach with a fresh example rather than repeating the same one.
SECTION 5

Teaching notes for the tricky spots

Homophones (a/à, et/est, on/ont, son/sont)

Teach the swap test, not memorisation: if you can swap the word for avait it's a; for étai(en)t it's est/sont; if you can say “and” it's et. This one rule unlocks most of them.

Feminine & plural exceptions

Don't front-load every exception. Teach the general rule first (add -e / add -s), get it solid, then introduce endings one family at a time (-eau→-eaux, -al→-aux…). The “7 words in -ou that take -x” is a fun memory challenge, not a test priority.

Verb endings

Anchor on the person → ending pattern (nous → -ons, ils/elles → -ent) rather than whole tables. Reuse one verb (aimer) across all tenses first so he hears the tense change, then swap in avoir/être/aller.

Agreement inside the noun group

Use the phrase “everyone in the group agrees” — determiner, adjective and noun all go singular/plural and masculine/feminine together. Have him change a whole GN to plural out loud.

SECTION 6

Checking progress

There's no account or grade book — keep it simple. For each topic, “ready” looks like:

  • ✅ Scores 4–5/5 on the quiz two rounds running, and
  • ✅ Can explain the rule back to you in his own words, and
  • ✅ Can write one correct original sentence using it.
